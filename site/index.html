Production, CASCADE, high risk. Press D, and Claude comes back with a safer plan.

Only what you need to decide.

Reading is the tiring part. A terminal makes you rebuild the context for every decision. Cockpit brings it with the question and leaves everything else out.

Only the decision

  • The question, the options and their trade-offs. You don't scroll back through 400 lines to see why it's asking.

Only the risk

  • The exact command, where it runs, and how hard it is to undo, highlighted before you press a key.

Only what changed

  • The plan's progress and the files it touched, grouped by folder. Auth and migrations are flagged.

Keep your terminal.

Already run Claude Code in tmux, iTerm or a plain terminal? Turn on Terminal sessions in Settings once. From then on, every permission prompt from those sessions also lands in Cockpit's queue, with the exact command, folder, repo and branch. Answer it in Cockpit or in the terminal, whichever comes first.

With Cockpit closed, the terminal asks as it always has, and nothing is ever allowed without you. Nothing to migrate. Works on macOS and Linux; not on Windows yet.

Five moments where Cockpit saves you the reading.

1

Everything that needs you, in one queue.

Questions, permissions and plan approvals from every session, oldest first. Press 1–3 to pick, A to allow, D to deny. The session carries on by itself.

2

One window per client.

Each workspace has its own Claude sign-in, memory and history, and shares your skills and settings. Switch with ⌘1–9. Press ⌘0 for the Overview: every workspace on one map, with what needs you counted beside its name. Click anything on it to jump straight in.

3

Review a PR one finding at a time.

Your /review comes back as findings, riskiest first, each with its snippet. Press N to note one, then publish every note as one GitHub review and approve. On your own PRs, Address does the reverse: each reviewer thread becomes a choice, and the session makes the fix.

4

Catch the command that sounds harmless.

Claude says "drop the old invoices table." The card shows the rest: it's production, and it's CASCADE. Press D, and Claude comes back with a safer plan instead of a restore from backup.

5

Plans you can read in ten seconds.

Ask Claude to plan before it codes and you get a one-page map: goal, scope, design, risks and open questions, each drawn the way it reads best. Mark it up, send every note at once, then hit Build from doc.

Questions

I only run one session at a time. Is it for me?

Probably yes, for different reasons: plans you can read in seconds, risky commands shown in full before you answer, and what changed grouped by folder.

How does Terminal sessions work, and what does it change?

It adds one hook to Claude Code's user settings (~/.claude/settings.json). When a terminal session asks for permission, the hook passes that request (the tool, the command, the folder) to Cockpit on your machine and waits; your terminal shows its prompt at the same time. It sees nothing else, apart from the session's log to notice when you've answered in the terminal. Turn it off and the file goes back exactly as it was.

Will more sessions use up my Claude plan faster?

Yes: every session uses your plan, exactly as in a terminal. Cockpit shows your 5-hour and weekly usage with when they reset, and you choose how many sessions may work at once.

How does it decide what's risky?

Plain rules you can read, not a model guessing: force-push, rm -rf, sudo, DROP TABLE, curl | sh, writes outside the project. See the rules.

Is it really free?

Yes. Cockpit is MIT licensed. Your sessions use your existing Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team or API), exactly as they do in a terminal.

Do my skills, commands, hooks and settings still work?

Yes. Cockpit runs your real Claude Code with your real setup. Workspaces keep memory and history separate per client while sharing your skills and settings.

What if a card gets something wrong?

The full transcript is one key away (⌘R), and every approval shows the exact command before it runs.

How is this different from Conductor or Vibe Kanban?

They help you run agents in parallel. Cockpit is about deciding: one queue across every session, one key per answer, the exact command and its risk in front of you. It runs locally on your own Claude Code, with no account and no cloud.

Why one git worktree per session? Don't they share my environment?

Each session gets its own checkout so edits never collide, while it uses your normal machine, tools and Claude setup. Like any fresh clone, a worktree may need its dependencies installed; ask the session, or note it in your project's CLAUDE.md. Prefer your own checkout? Turn worktrees off when you start a session. Review and Address always run in your existing clone.

Is this made by Anthropic?

No. Cockpit is an independent open-source project built on Anthropic's public Agent SDK. "Claude" and "Claude Code" are trademarks of Anthropic.

Can I build it or contribute?

Please do: git clone https://github.com/getcockpit/cockpit && ./scripts/dev.sh. See CONTRIBUTING.md.
