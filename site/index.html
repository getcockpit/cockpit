I got tired of reading.

Most of my coding now happens through Claude Code, usually several sessions at once. Each one produces a lot of text, and my job had quietly become scrolling through it to find the one question that was waiting for me.

That reminded me of Minority Report, where nobody reads logs. They look at the situation and decide. So I started looking at interfaces built for that, and the ones that stuck with me came from air traffic and flight control. They're designed for people who can't afford to miss anything: everything stays quiet until something needs you.

That idea became Cockpit. The difference I didn't expect was how much less tired I was. Each decision arrives with only the context it needs, so I'm not rebuilding it from scrollback every time.

I compared my first four days with Cockpit against the three weeks before. I went from about 7 sessions a day to about 70, and from about 4 running at once to 11 (14 on the busiest day). Most of that went into building Cockpit with Cockpit, so it isn't a clean experiment. But it's the most I've ever had going at once without losing track.
— Bohdan Kostko, who made it

What it does

Cockpit drives the Claude Code you already use. Your sessions run as they always do; Cockpit changes what you have to look at.

One queue for everything that needs you

Questions, permissions and plan approvals from every session, oldest first, each answered with a single key: 1–3 to pick, A to allow, D to deny. The session carries on by itself.

Risky commands stop in red

You see exactly what will run and where. A "cleanup" that turns out to be a DROP TABLE … CASCADE on production is hard to miss. Deny it and Claude comes back with a safer plan.

Plans as a one-page map, not chat history

Ask Claude to plan before it codes and you get goal, scope, design, risks and open questions on one page. Mark it up, send your notes at once, then build from it.

A timeline of who needs you next

What every session did, what it's doing now, and which one will want you in the next few minutes.

Where each session stands

A plan with live progress and the files it touched, grouped by folder, with auth and migrations flagged.

Pull request reviews, one finding at a time

The riskiest snippets come first. Press one key to turn a finding into a review comment, or go the other way and turn reviewer threads into fixes.

Your Claude, on your machine

Cockpit runs Claude Code through Anthropic's official Agent SDK, signed in as you. Your skills, hooks and settings keep working.

  • It never touches your login. No tokens are read or stored.
  • There's no server. History stays in a local file on your computer.
  • You can leave any time. Hand a session to a real terminal with ⌘T.
  • It costs nothing. MIT licensed, and it uses the plan you already pay for.

Install it

You need Claude Code, signed in once. On macOS or Linux, one line does the rest:

# 1. Claude Code, if you don't have it yet
$ curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash && claude

# 2. Cockpit (macOS or Linux, no security prompts)
$ curl -fsSL https://getcockpit.github.io/install.sh | bash

Or download it for your system:

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Cockpit is MIT licensed. Your sessions use your existing Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team or API), exactly as they do in a terminal.

Do my skills, commands, hooks and settings still work?

Yes. Cockpit runs your real Claude Code with your real setup. Workspaces keep memory and history separate per client while sharing your skills and settings.

What if a card gets something wrong?

The full transcript is one key away (⌘R), and every approval shows the exact command before it runs.

How finished is it?

Not very. It's young and rough in places, and I use it every day, so it changes fast. If something breaks, open an issue.

Is this made by Anthropic?

No. Cockpit is an independent open-source project built on Anthropic's public Agent SDK. "Claude" and "Claude Code" are trademarks of Anthropic.

Can I build it or contribute?

Please do: git clone https://github.com/getcockpit/cockpit && ./scripts/dev.sh. See CONTRIBUTING.md.
