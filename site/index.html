Claude Code in Cockpit

Production, CASCADE, high risk. Press D and Claude comes back with a safer plan.

You still make every call. You just stop hunting for them.

Faster answers only help if they're the right ones. So Cockpit doesn't summarise a risky command for you - it shows the real one, and tells you why it's flagged.

The exact command

  • Not a paraphrase. The full command, where it runs, and what it touches. The transcript is one key away (⌘R) if you want more.

Plain rules, not a guess

  • Risk comes from rules you can read: force-push, rm -rf, sudo, DROP TABLE, curl | sh, writes outside the project. No model deciding what's safe. See the rules

You set the bar

  • Ask only for risky stuff (the default), ask for every command, or ask for everything. Change it any time. Deny is always the first key on a high-risk card.

A morning with eight sessions.

Four moments where Cockpit saves you the reading. It's useful with one session, and it earns its keep with five.

1

Everything that needs you, in one queue.

Questions, permissions and plan approvals from every session, oldest first. Press 1–3 to pick, A to allow, D to deny. The session carries on by itself, and nothing sits waiting in tab six.

2

A plan you can read in ten seconds.

Pick "plan it first" and Claude writes one page before touching code: the goal, what's in and out, the risks, and the questions it needs you to answer. Answer them inline, leave notes, then hit Build from plan.

3

PR reviews, one finding at a time.

Your /review comes back as findings, riskiest first, each with its snippet. Press N to note one, then publish them as one GitHub review. On your own PRs it runs the other way: each reviewer comment becomes a choice, and the session makes the fix.

4

One workspace per client.

Each workspace has its own Claude sign-in, memory and history, and shares your skills and settings. ⌘1–9 to switch, ⌘0 to see all of them on one map, with what needs you counted beside each name.

Up and running in 2 minutes.

You need Claude Code, signed in. Don't have it yet? Step 1 below.

# 1. Claude Code, signed in once (skip if you have it)
$ curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash && claude

# 2. Cockpit: macOS or Linux
$ curl -fsSL https://getcockpit.github.io/install.sh | bash

It downloads the latest release from GitHub and puts it in Applications. Read the script - it's short.

Questions

I only run one session at a time. Is it for me?

Probably yes, just for different reasons: plans you can read in seconds, risky commands shown in full before you answer, and what changed grouped by folder. And once waiting on sessions stops being a chore, a second and third one get a lot easier.

Won't one-key answers make me rubber-stamp things?

That's the risk we design against. You choose when you're asked, the card shows the real command and why it's flagged, and Deny is the first key on anything high risk. The point is to spend your attention on the calls that matter, not on finding them.

Does it replace my terminal or tmux setup?

No. It runs your real Claude Code, with your skills, hooks and settings. Any session can go to a real terminal with ⌘T, and the raw transcript is always ⌘R.

Will more sessions burn through my plan limits?

Sessions use your plan exactly as they would in a terminal, nothing extra. Running more at once uses more, so your 5-hour and weekly usage sits in the header, and you can cap how many work at once in Settings.

Is it really free?

Yes. MIT licensed, no account, no paid tier. Sessions run on your existing Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team or API).

How is this different from Conductor or Vibe Kanban?

They help you run agents in parallel. Cockpit is about deciding: one queue across every session, the exact command and its risk in front of you, one key per answer. It runs locally on your own Claude Code, with no account and no cloud.

Why one git worktree per session?

So edits never collide. Each session gets its own checkout and uses your normal machine, tools and Claude setup. Like any fresh clone, a worktree may need its dependencies installed; ask the session, or note it in your CLAUDE.md. Prefer your own checkout? Turn worktrees off when you start a session.

Is this made by Anthropic?

No. Cockpit is an independent open-source project built on Anthropic's public Agent SDK. "Claude" and "Claude Code" are trademarks of Anthropic.

Can I build it or contribute?

Please do: git clone https://github.com/getcockpit/cockpit && ./scripts/dev.sh. See CONTRIBUTING.md.
